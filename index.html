Mathematical Derivation Appendix: Sensitivity & Budget Allocation

Plain-English breakdown of Differential Privacy principles, medical sensitivity calculations, Laplace noise calibration, and privacy budget sharing across patient tests.

Sensitivity: How Much Can One Patient Change the Data?

Core DP Concept

"Imagine two clinical datasets that are identical, except one person is added or removed. Sensitivity measures the maximum change this single person can cause to any medical average."

Sensitivity = (Maximum Value − Minimum Value) ⁄ Total Patients (5,000)

Because there are 5,000 patients in the dataset, one individual's effect on any test average is diluted down to less than 1/5,000th of the possible range.

Formal Mathematical Definition:
Let D, D' ∈ 𝒟 be neighboring databases (‖D ⊕ D'‖ = 1) with N = 5,000 samples.
For a scalar query f_i(D) = (1/N) Σ_{k=1}^N x_{k,i} bounded by [a_i, b_i]:
Δf_i = max ‖f_i(D) - f_i(D')‖₁ = (b_i - a_i) / N

Adding Controlled Noise (Laplace Shield)

Laplace Mechanism

"To prevent someone from figuring out who was in the dataset, we add a calculated amount of random statistical noise to each clinical test result."

Noise Amount = Feature Sensitivity ⁄ Privacy Budget (ε)
🔒 Smaller ε (stricter privacy):

More noise is added to mask identities, making it mathematically impossible to isolate any patient.

🔓 Larger ε (looser privacy):

Less noise is added, keeping numbers closer to raw medical values for diagnostic research.

Formal Laplace Probability Density:
M_i(D) = f_i(D) + Lap(0, Δf_i / ε_i), where scale parameter b_i = Δf_i / ε_i.
PDF: f(x) = (1 / 2b_i) · exp(-|x| / b_i). Satisfies pure ε-Differential Privacy (δ = 0).

Sharing the Privacy Budget Across 16 Patient Tests

Sequential Composition

"Every time we look at a test (like blood pressure or glucose), we spend a portion of our total privacy budget. The total budget is the sum of what we spend on each test."

Total Privacy Budget Spent = Sum of Budgets Across All 16 Clinical Tests

Critical life-saving markers (such as Creatinine and Oxygen SpO₂) receive a slightly larger share of the budget so the model remains sharp at detecting heart and kidney failure.

Sequential Composition Theorem:
ε_total = Σ_{i=1}^{16} ε_i ⟹ ε_i = ε_total · (w_i / Σ w_j)
Weighted allocation assigns higher budget weights w_i to diagnostic-critical bio-markers.
